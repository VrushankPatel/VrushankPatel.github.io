Kubernetes StatefulSet Gotchas in Production

Hard-won operational understanding from backend infrastructure, distributed systems, performance engineering and production reliability.

KubernetesReliabilityStorageOperations

The ordered-pod abstraction is only the beginning

StatefulSets give workloads stable identity and predictable ordering, but production behavior is dominated by storage, disruption handling, node lifecycle and recovery semantics.

Storage creates hard topology constraints

Local NVMe can deliver excellent latency and IOPS, but it couples a workload to a node. Persistent volumes, CSI behavior and broker identity therefore become part of the application's availability model.

Drains, PDBs and rolling upgrades interact

PodDisruptionBudgets can protect availability while simultaneously preventing the autoscaler or operator from completing a drain. Ordered rollouts improve safety but can lengthen maintenance windows.

Kafka adds another layer of correctness

Broker-to-partition affinity, leader handoff and stable DNS names matter during restarts. Graceful pre-stop behavior and topology-aware scheduling reduce the chance that infrastructure automation becomes application instability.

The operational lesson

Stateful workloads need a tested lifecycle: startup, steady state, rolling update, node drain, storage failure and recovery. The happy path of a StatefulSet YAML is not the production design.

Good architecture makes correctness obvious, failure modes explicit, performance measurable and operations boring.
